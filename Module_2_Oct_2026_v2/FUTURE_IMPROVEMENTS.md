## How the sessions ran

The materials were not followed exactly. The slides and worksheets were used intermittently, as it made sense in the room. Plan the next version so trainers can pick and choose, with clear stand-alone pieces.

## AI sessions

- **Hands-on AI analysis session (new).** Give participants the GreenWaste data and have them use AI to replicate the results we report, for example a difference-in-differences estimate, first in a naive way and then with a more advanced prompt. This should be more engaging than critiquing a prepared response, because participants see the difference a better prompt makes on their own data. Decide where it sits (after Day 2 methods is the natural place) and how participants check the AI's numbers against the course results.
- **Keep the live "naive prompt, then better prompt" pattern.** In the charts session the trainer ran a basic prompt and then a better one (the AI asks questions about the data first, clear labels, a simple comparison of two groups) and discussed the output. Consider reusing this in other sessions.
- **Reconcile the materials with what was delivered.** The Day 3 S2 deck and worksheet (page 5 and the Copilot slide) still describe the prepared AI response. The Day 4 S1-S2 materials still reflect the earlier plan. Update the sources, worksheets and trainer keys, then re-render, as the README publishing steps require.
- **Write down the prompts used.** Save the basic and improved charts prompts, and the comms-guidelines and AI pet-peeves prompt used for the brief, as a handout or an appendix to the AI good practice card, so participants can reuse them.
- **Check access and data handling.** Decide in advance which AI tool participants use and whether they need accounts. Use only the fictional GreenWaste data, not real identifiers.

## Examples and realism

- **More examples, and less GreenWaste.** Participants were tired of GreenWaste by the end. Use a wider set of cases.
- **Use a real evaluation report and real regression tables.** Even if they are more complicated, they are more tangible and connect to what participants may see online. Open question to settle: how do we keep it real and work with real data, but not too technical?
- **Possible answer: AI with the room.** Share the screen while we work with the AI, and take suggestions from the room on what to ask next. This could work especially well for the session on interviewing the evaluator.
- **Find out what participants work on.** Ask about the problems and programmes participants are working on, perhaps in an introduction at the start. Add a poll with a word cloud that shows their replies, giving a live list of programme examples. Use these examples in later sessions.

## Format and interactivity

- **More polls** throughout the sessions.
- **Spend more time on the materials.** Make them really nice, clear and interactive, not homework-like. Participants have laptops and can work on them in the session.
