## Exercise 1 · Menti poll, opening commit

Appears at minute 3, slide 2. Type: live vote.

Question: "A programme reports that traffic fatalities fell by 30% in one year.
Would you fund a second year?"

Options: Yes · No · Can't tell without more information.

What the partner builds: one Menti slide, three options, no correct answer
revealed. Add a second Menti slide with the same question, used at minute 12 to
show whether answers moved once the comparison group appeared.

## Exercise 2 · Menti poll, p-value prediction

Appears at minute 50, slide 20. Type: numeric estimate.

Question: "We shuffled who got the programme 1,000 times and let it do nothing.
Out of 1,000 shuffles, how many produce a difference at least as large as the
one we measured?"

Options: 5 · 25 · 100 · 400 · 900.

What the partner builds: one Menti slide. The answer is about 23 of 1,000, which
is p = 0.023. Do not reveal it before the room has guessed.

## Exercise 3 · Terms bingo

Appears at minute 76, slide 30. Type: printed card plus live read-aloud.

What the partner builds: one card per participant, A5, printed both sides.

Front: a 3 by 3 grid holding the five session terms plus four distractors. The
grid cells are Mean, Comparison group, Treatment effect, Coefficient, p-value,
Confidence interval, Significance, Sample size, Baseline.

Back: the three questions. "Compared to what?" · "How big is it, in units that
matter to the decision?" · "How sure are we?" Add a line noting that Day 2 adds
a fourth: who does the result apply to.

The eight excerpts below are read aloud, one every 30 to 45 seconds. The slide
shows them, so the card does not need to carry the text.

1. "Waste costs in participating businesses fell 30% over the programme period."
2. "The programme effect is 1,240 AED, with a 95% confidence interval of 210 to 2,270."
3. "The improvement is statistically significant (p = 0.008), so the programme should be scaled nationally."
4. "Costs fell 420 AED in the programme group while rising 90 AED in the comparison group."
5. "Across the sample, the average business saved 1,449 AED."
6. "With 40 businesses in the study, no effect was detected."
7. "The coefficient on class size is -0.32, so reducing class size raises pass rates."
8. "Costs fell 420 AED against a rise of 90 AED in the comparison group. The programme must cut costs by at least 300 AED to justify scale-up."

The two judgement calls, run after the bingo round. Both use the three questions.

- Excerpt 3: do not act. The p-value says the effect is probably not zero. It
  says nothing about whether the effect is large enough to matter.
- Excerpt 8: act. Costs fell 420 AED while the comparison rose 90, so the effect
  is 510 AED, which clears the stated 300 AED bar.

## Exercise 4 · AI prompt cards

Appears at minute 66, slide 26. Type: group work with printed prompt cards.

What the partner builds: four cards, A6. Each carries one prompt on the front
and the reveal on the back.

| Card | Prompt | Reveal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain what a 95% confidence interval means, in one paragraph, for someone who is not a statistician. | Expect the claim that there is a 95% probability the true value lies inside this one interval. The correct reading is about the method: 95 of 100 repeat studies would catch the true value. |
| 2 | Explain what a p-value means. | Expect "the probability the result is due to chance". That is not what it is. |
| 3 | School pass rates rose 5 percentage points after our programme. Is that a good result? | The answer usually arrives without asking what the pass rate is compared to, or what bar the programme must clear. |
| 4 | The coefficient on class size is -0.32. What should I do about it? | Look for a recommendation with no interval. Without the interval, the model cannot tell you whether the relationship is real. |

Group instructions, printed on the card: run the prompt, mark anything the
answer gets wrong or overstates, then compare with the reveal on the back.
